Privacy policy: Nival browser extension

Last updated October 12, 2026

The Nival browser extension works together with Nival Shell, a desktop shell for Windows, on the same computer. It lets Nival Shell's assistant fill your saved logins and payment cards on the right website without the assistant ever seeing them.

What the extension reads

Only when Nival Shell asks (when it is about to fill a field), and only in the tab you're using:

  • the address of the page, and of the frame within it, that has the keyboard focus;
  • whether the focused field takes text, and whether it is a password field.

What it does with it

It gives that information to Nival Shell on the same computer, through the browser's native messaging, and to nothing else. Nival Shell uses it to check that a login is filled only on its own site and that a password goes only into a password field. Before a card's number is typed, the extension covers the field on the page so the digits don't appear in screenshots; the cover goes when the field is emptied, or after 15 minutes.

What it never does

  • It never receives your passwords, one-time codes or card details. Nival Shell keeps them on your computer, encrypted by Windows for your account, and types them itself.
  • It sends nothing to the internet, to Nival, or to anyone else. It has no server and no analytics.
  • It keeps nothing: it stores no data in the browser and keeps no history.
  • It doesn't read or change any page unless Nival Shell asks, as described above.

Sharing

We don't sell, share or transfer your data, and don't use it for advertising, for anything other than filling your logins and cards, or to determine creditworthiness.

Removing it

Nival Shell adds the extension to your browsers and takes it out again when Nival Shell is uninstalled, or from Settings › Passwords › Browsers in Nival Shell.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with its date above.
